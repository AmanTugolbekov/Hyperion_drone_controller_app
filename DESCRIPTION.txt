Hyperion — Drone Controller App
===============================

A mobile ground station for the Hyperion smart drone, and for Wi-Fi
quadcopters in general. Written in Flutter, running on Android, and built at
Ala-Too International University.


THE WIDER PROJECT

Hyperion is a smart drone that picks up, carries and releases lightweight
objects using a gripping mechanism, so that small items can be moved without a
person present — deliveries, collection tasks, and places that are awkward to
reach.

That aircraft needs a pilot interface. This repository is that interface: the
part the operator actually holds. It also works as a standalone replacement for
the stock apps shipped with cheap Wi-Fi drones, which is how it is being
developed and tested today.


WHAT THE CONTROLLER DOES

Flight control
  Two on-screen sticks for throttle, yaw, roll and pitch, with a switchable
  layout for Mode 1 and Mode 2 pilots. Take-off, landing, emergency stop,
  360-degree flip and headless mode. Per-axis trim to cancel drift, gyro
  calibration, and three rate settings at 30, 60 and 100 percent. The phone's
  accelerometer can replace the right stick for tilt flying.

Camera
  The drone's live view fills the screen, with stills saved to the phone's
  gallery. The feed is rotated automatically: the camera is mounted sideways,
  so the drone reports its stream as a portrait image.

Telemetry and diagnostics
  Battery, altitude and link state, plus a diagnostics screen that exposes the
  raw exchange with the aircraft and allows the address and ports to be set by
  hand. When something does not work, the app shows why rather than failing
  silently.

Languages
  Russian, English and Kyrgyz.


HOW IT TALKS TO THE AIRCRAFT

Each drone family is a plugin behind one interface, so supporting a new
aircraft means writing a single class. The E88 family is implemented and
verified against real hardware: control runs over UDP port 7099 at twenty-five
frames per second, and the camera is an RTSP MJPEG stream on port 7070 that is
decoded with FFmpeg.

That protocol was not guessed. Published reverse-engineering notes for this
drone family disagree with each other about both the control port and the video
transport, and their authors state they never verified against hardware — two
attempts built from those notes did not work. The answer came from reading the
stock application's own code, and was then confirmed against the live sockets
the stock app opens while flying.

Two platform details matter more than they appear to. Android treats the
drone's access point as a dead network, because it offers no internet, and
quietly routes every packet over mobile data instead; the app pins its sockets
to Wi-Fi to prevent that. And some boards in this family boot bound to their
physical remote and discard Wi-Fi commands until explicitly switched over.
Either one, left unhandled, looks exactly like a drone that is simply ignoring
the pilot.


STATUS

Control and camera are confirmed working on real hardware. The remaining
protocols compile but have not been flown. Video recording, telemetry decoding
for this drone family, and the iOS build are still outstanding.
